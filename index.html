The honest part

Three things quietly put a recovery grant at risk

Recovery is one of the hardest categories Google polices. That's not a reason to avoid the grant — it's a reason to have someone watching it who knows exactly where the tripwires are.

Risk one

The 5% click-through rule

Google requires Ad Grant accounts to keep a 5% click-through rate every month. Miss it two months in a row and the account can be suspended. Most recovery accounts fail this because they're bidding on broad, curious searches instead of the searches people make when they're ready to pick up the phone.

Risk two

Healthcare ad policy & LegitScript

In the U.S., organizations advertising addiction treatment services generally need LegitScript certification before Google will let those ads run, plus Google's own certification on top of it. Ads that run without it get disapproved — and repeated disapprovals are a bad look on an account you can't afford to lose.

Risk three

Keyword hygiene

No single-word keywords. No overly generic terms. Every keyword has to carry a Quality Score of 3 or better. A well-meaning volunteer adding "addiction" as a keyword can drag an entire account under the line in a matter of weeks.

Also required

Account structure Google actually accepts

At least two ad groups in every campaign, at least two active ads in every ad group, and sitelink extensions in place. It sounds fussy. It's the difference between an account that survives review and one that doesn't.

Also required

Conversion tracking that means something

Google wants to see that the clicks lead somewhere real — a call placed, an intake form completed, an insurance verification requested. We set this up so you can point at a number in a board meeting and say what it bought.

Before we go further

Not every recovery organization qualifies

I'd rather tell you this in minute two than in month two. Google excludes several categories from the Ad Grant program outright, and recovery organizations sit close to some of those lines.

Usually a good fit

  • 501(c)(3) nonprofit recovery and treatment organizations
  • Sober living and transitional housing nonprofits
  • Peer recovery support and community coalitions
  • Family support and intervention nonprofits
  • Faith-based recovery programs open to the whole community
  • Prevention and harm-reduction nonprofits

Generally excluded by Google

  • Hospitals and hospital-operated treatment programs
  • Formal healthcare organizations and medical groups
  • Government entities and government-run programs
  • Schools, districts, and universities (their philanthropic arms may qualify)
  • For-profit treatment centers, regardless of mission

If you're not sure which side of the line you're on — and plenty of recovery organizations genuinely aren't — that's the first thing we check on the call, before anyone talks about strategy.

Plain-English answers

The questions I get asked most

Is the $10,000 real money?

It's real advertising, not a check. Google gives approved nonprofits up to $10,000 per month in free search ads. You can't move it to another budget line, and unused amounts don't roll over — anything you don't use each month is simply gone.

What is the 5% rule, exactly?

Google requires Ad Grant accounts to maintain at least a 5% click-through rate — meaning at least five out of every hundred people who see your ad click it. Fall below it for two consecutive months and Google can suspend the account. It's the single most common reason recovery grants get switched off.

Do we need LegitScript certification?

In the U.S., addiction treatment providers generally need LegitScript certification, followed by Google's own certification, before ads for treatment services will run. Requirements change and vary by country, so we confirm your exact situation during the audit rather than guessing. If you need to start the process, we'll walk you through it.

Can a suspended account be brought back?

Often, yes. Suspension isn't always permanent — most policy suspensions can be appealed once the underlying problem is fixed. Bring us the account and we'll tell you honestly what we think the odds are.

Will this replace the paid ads we already run?

Not usually. Grant ads sit below paid ads in the auction, so they capture the searches paid budget doesn't reach. Most of our clients keep both and let the grant carry the volume while paid dollars go to the most competitive terms.

How long until we see intake calls?

If you already have an active grant account, usually within the first four to six weeks. If we're applying from scratch, add roughly four to eight weeks for Google for Nonprofits verification and approval before anything runs.

How much of our staff time does this take?

About an hour to get started, and a 30-minute call each month if you want it. You're already wearing enough hats. That's the whole point of hiring us.
