## Platform

web

## Users

Primary users are decision-makers and doers at independent animal-welfare 501(c)(3) nonprofits in the United States, especially organizations with annual budgets in the $500K–$5M range.

Typical roles:
- Executive director wearing multiple hats
- Operations or development director who is also the “marketing team”
- Foster/volunteer coordinator managing communications on top of animal care
- Small-staff sanctuary or rescue team with no dedicated digital marketer

Secondary users: municipal or government-run shelters needing guidance on what they *can* do (they are excluded from the Google Ad Grant, so the site must direct them honestly).

## Product Purpose

SecondChance Media helps animal-welfare nonprofits get found online by the people ready to adopt, volunteer, foster, transport, or donate. It turns underused or intimidating marketing channels—especially the Google Ad Grant—into reliable, manageable engines for the organization’s mission.

Success means the nonprofit consistently claims and keeps its $10,000/month Google Ad Grant, turns that traffic into real actions (adoptions, foster applications, volunteer signups, donations), and has a website and tracking system the staff can understand.

## Positioning

SecondChance Media is a digital marketing partner *only* for animal nonprofits—not a generalist agency that happens to take nonprofit clients. Its differentiator is category depth: it understands the grant rules, the animal-welfare operating reality (foster networks, sanctuaries, TNR, transport), and the emotional decision-making of people searching for animals to help.

The practice is founder-led by Melanie Deck and speaks in a direct, plain-English, story-first voice rather than agency jargon.

## Operating Context

- Visitors usually arrive by word-of-mouth, social proof, or search while already overwhelmed by operations.
- The core offer is a high-trust, consultative relationship, not a self-serve tool.
- Buying cycles are constrained by nonprofit calendars, board approval, grant cycles, and limited staff time.
- The Google Ad Grant has strict, technical compliance rules that nonprofits commonly drift out of without realizing it.
- Website conversions are adoption inquiries, foster applications, volunteer signups, and donations.
- Work mixes done-for-you services with knowledge transfer so the organization is not permanently dependent.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Confirmed capabilities:
- Google Ad Grant application and ongoing compliance management
- Paid search layered on top of the grant for urgent or campaign needs
- Website redesign focused on mobile-first conversion
- Local SEO
- Social media
- Conversion tracking and reporting
- Training and knowledge transfer for staff/volunteers

Confirmed constraints:
- Only registered 501(c)(3) animal-welfare organizations are eligible for the core Google Ad Grant offer
- Government-run animal control is excluded from the grant
- No hidden pricing; every price is published
- Free eligibility check before any paid engagement

Open decisions:
- Visual brand direction (name, mark, and palette are open to refinement)
- Exact pricing tiers and package boundaries
- Real case studies/testimonials (currently placeholders; must not be fabricated)

## Product Principles

1. **Mission first, marketing second.** Every recommendation must serve the animals and the people caring for them, not vanity metrics.
2. **Plain English over jargon.** Explain grants, compliance, and campaigns the way a rescue director would explain them to a volunteer.
3. **Honest eligibility.** Do not sell services to organizations that cannot use them; redirect them to the right path.
4. **Empower, don’t dependency-trap.** Build knowledge inside the organization so marketing survives staff turnover.
5. **Mobile-first and conversion-first.** Most supporters will find the nonprofit on a phone while emotionally mid-decision.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- No product-specific accessibility standard has been confirmed, but the public site should meet WCAG 2.1 AA as a baseline because nonprofit audiences include volunteers, donors, and adopters of varying abilities and devices.
